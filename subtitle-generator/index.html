How to use Multilingual Subtitle Generator

  1. Load a video or audio file with clear speech.
  2. Read the model notice and opt in to download and run local Whisper tiny recognition.
  3. Wait while the browser processes the audio; keep the tab active.
  4. Review the editable transcript and correct names, numbers or misheard words.
  5. Adjust the start and end times where a caption appears too early or too late.
  6. Export SRT, VTT or plain TXT and keep the original media file.

In-depth guide

Captions generated on your own device

Adding captions usually means either typing them by hand or sending a recording to a transcription service. This tool offers a third route: Whisper tiny speech recognition running locally through Transformers.js in your browser. Load a video or audio file, opt in to the model download and the page produces an editable transcript with timed lines. You can then correct what it heard and export SRT, VTT or plain TXT. Your media is never uploaded. The trade-off is honest and visible before you start: the model and its support files are around 75 to 80 MB on first use, and running recognition takes time and processing power. There is no promise of studio-grade accuracy, because speech recognition depends heavily on the recording. What you get is a useful first draft that stays on your device and can be reviewed line by line.

Why opt in to the model?

The recognition model is an additional component, not part of the basic page. Loading it means a sizeable download and a period of local computation, so the tool asks before fetching anything. That keeps the rest of the page light for visitors who only want to read about captions. Once you agree, the model is cached by the browser, which usually makes later sessions quicker. You remain in control: clearing site data removes the cached files, and you can decide whether the download is worth it for a single short clip. A quick test on a few minutes of representative audio is a sensible first step. If the result is usable, continue with the full recording; if the source is too noisy or the language is poorly handled, you have lost only a little time and can choose another approach.

Preparing audio for better recognition

Speech recognition works from sound, so the quality of the recording sets the ceiling on the transcript. One speaker at a time, a close microphone and a quiet room all help. Background music, traffic, echoes and several people talking over each other cause words to be missed or invented. Proper names, product names and numbers are especially fragile because the model has no way to know your preferred spelling. If you can influence the recording, do so before transcribing rather than after. For an existing file, listen to a short sample and consider whether a cleaner version exists. The tool does not enhance noisy audio first; it presents what the model heard. After recognition, read through the transcript while playing the matching section of the media. Correcting a handful of names and timings is usually much faster than starting from an empty document.

Editing the timed transcript

A transcript that looks right is not automatically good subtitles. Captions need to appear and disappear at sensible moments, stay short enough to read, and match what is actually on screen. This tool groups recognition into timed lines and lets you edit each line's start, end and text. If a line appears half a second early, change its timing rather than re-transcribing the whole file. Split long sentences into readable lines and avoid dumping a full paragraph on screen at once. The editor is deliberately straightforward: it does not expose individual word timings, so very fine synchronisation needs manual adjustment. Use the playback in your media player as the final reference, because subtitle timing is judged against the finished picture, not against the transcription preview alone.

Export formats, limits and privacy

SRT is the most widely accepted subtitle format and is a good default. VTT is useful for web video players and supports a few extra conventions, while TXT gives you a plain readable transcript without timing information. Choose the format that matches where the captions will be used, and keep the original media so you can check the export in context. This is a single-file transcription tool: it does not translate captions into another language, mix them into the video or manage a multi-episode project. Those jobs need a full editor or a separate workflow. Privacy is simple throughout. Recognition happens locally, the model is the only download, and your media stays in memory until you close or reload the page. Export the file you need before leaving, and remember to review it; automatic captions are a starting point, not a finished publication.

Frequently asked questions

Is my audio sent to a transcription service?

No. Recognition runs in your browser with a locally loaded model. The media is never uploaded to LetitEdit or to a speech API. The one network download is the model and its support files, which come to your device when you opt in.

Why is the first run slower?

Whisper tiny and its supporting files are roughly 75 to 80 MB, so the first use involves a noticeable download before transcription can begin. Later runs may reuse the browser cache. The page tells you what is happening rather than appearing to do nothing.

How accurate will the captions be?

It depends on the recording. Clear speech, a single speaker and low background noise give the best results. Accents, overlapping voices, music and distant microphones cause mistakes. There is no guaranteed accuracy figure, so always read through the transcript and fix errors before publishing.

Which languages are supported?

Whisper is multilingual, but performance varies by language and recording conditions. The tool does not promise perfect results for every language. Test a short sample first if the language is less commonly represented in speech models, and be ready to edit the output.

How do I edit the timed lines?

Recognition groups the transcript into short, timed lines. Each line has a start and end time you can edit in the text box, and you can correct misheard words there too. This build does not expose individual word timings, so very fine synchronisation needs manual adjustment or a dedicated subtitle editor.

What happens to the model and my file afterwards?

The model is cached by your browser and can be removed with site data controls. Your audio or video is held in memory only while the page is open; reloading or closing clears it. Export your SRT, VTT or TXT before leaving, and keep the original media as your reference when checking timings.
