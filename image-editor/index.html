In-depth guide

A lightweight editor for everyday photos

Most photo jobs are small: a crooked horizon, a dim exposure, a caption that needs to sit in the corner. This editor collects those everyday corrections in one place so you can finish them without installing software or handing a file to a remote service. You load an image, trim it with a crop slider, fix the orientation with rotate and flip, then tune brightness, contrast and saturation until the picture looks right. Blur, grayscale and sepia are available for stylistic changes, and a text overlay lets you label a screenshot or mark up a shared photo. The preview updates as you work, so each adjustment is easy to judge before you commit. When you are satisfied, you download the result in PNG, JPG or WebP. The original file on your device is never modified.

The workflow from upload to export

Start by uploading a JPG, PNG or WebP image from your device. The editor decodes it locally and places it in the preview area. Next, drag the crop slider to choose how much of the frame to keep; cropping early reduces the area that later filters have to process. If the picture is sideways or mirrored, apply rotate or flip before anything else, because text and colour decisions are easier once the orientation is correct. Then work through the adjustments in small steps rather than pushing a single slider to its limit. Add a text overlay last, position it over a clear part of the image, and check that it remains readable. Finally, pick an export format and download. If a result disappoints you, reset the adjustments and try again.

Understanding the adjustments

Brightness lifts or lowers the overall lightness of every pixel, which helps with an underexposed shot but can wash out highlights if pushed too far. Contrast widens or narrows the gap between light and dark tones, making a flat image look punchier. Saturation controls the intensity of colour; reducing it moves a photo toward grayscale, while increasing it can make skies and clothing look unnaturally vivid. Blur softens detail and is useful for de-emphasising a busy background, though it cannot recover information that is out of focus. Grayscale removes colour entirely, and sepia applies a warm brown tint for an older look. These controls are deliberately simple, so combine them gently and compare against the original preview rather than trusting a single number.

Choosing the right export format

PNG is lossless, which means it preserves the exact pixels you see in the preview. Choose it when your overlay contains small text, sharp lines or flat colour, because compression artefacts are most visible around lettering. JPG is lossy and produces much smaller files for photographs with smooth gradients and natural texture; it is a good default for sharing a photo where a few extra kilobytes matter. WebP sits between the two in many cases, offering efficient compression and support for transparency in current browsers. If a downloaded file looks softer than the preview, switch to PNG and compare again. Remember that re-saving a JPG a second time adds another generation of loss, so keep an original copy and export from that rather than from a previous download.

Privacy, limits and troubleshooting

Editing happens in your browser, so your photo is not uploaded to a server and no account is required. That also means the page cannot reach files stored elsewhere in your cloud drive unless you download them first. This is a single-image editor for quick corrections, not a layer-based design application: there are no masks, brush tools or undo history beyond what the preview offers. Very large files may take longer to decode and render, especially on older devices. If the page becomes sluggish, crop the image first, close other tabs and retry with a smaller file. If an overlay disappears or a slider seems to have no effect, reset the tool and reload the image, then apply changes one at a time so you can see which step causes the problem.

Frequently asked questions

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The image is decoded and edited by JavaScript running in your browser. Nothing is sent to LetitEdit or to a third-party image service, and closing the page clears the working copy.

What can I actually change here?

You can crop with a slider, rotate and flip, adjust brightness, contrast, saturation and blur, apply grayscale or sepia, and add a text overlay. It is meant for quick corrections rather than layered design work.

Which formats can I export?

PNG, JPG and WebP. PNG keeps sharp text and flat colour, JPG produces smaller photo files, and WebP offers a good size and quality balance where it is supported.

Why does my export look different from the preview?

JPG and WebP are lossy, so fine gradients or small text can soften slightly after saving. Choose PNG when you need pixel-exact output, and re-check the preview after changing format.

This tool feels slow with a large photo. What can I do?

Very high-resolution files can take a moment to process in any browser. Crop first to reduce the working area, close other heavy tabs, and try a smaller source image if the page becomes unresponsive.
