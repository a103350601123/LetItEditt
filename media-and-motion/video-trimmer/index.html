Guide

What video trimming means

Trimming is the simplest edit you can make to a video: you choose a start point and an end point, and everything outside that range is removed. It is different from cropping, which changes the frame, and from compressing, which changes the bitrate. A trim changes the duration while keeping the picture and sound that fall inside the range.

This tool offers two ways to use that range. Keep selected section exports only the highlighted part. Remove selected section cuts the highlighted part out and joins the material before and after it. Both modes produce a new file; the original on your device is never modified.

How to trim a video

  1. Add a video. Drag a file onto the upload area or choose Browse video. MP4, WebM, MOV, M4V and MKV are accepted when the browser can decode them.
  2. Find the section. Play the preview, drag the seek bar, or use the arrow keys. The timeline playhead follows playback.
  3. Set the boundaries. Drag the blue start and end handles, type exact times, or press Set to playhead. Zoom the timeline when you need more room to place a cut.
  4. Choose a mode. Keep the highlighted range, or remove it and join the remaining parts.
  5. Preview the result. Preview result plays the selection in keep mode, or skips the selected range in remove mode, so you can check the cut before exporting.
  6. Export. Pick MP4 or WebM and a quality level, then export. The tool reports real progress, verifies the output, and only then offers the download.

How start and end times work

The start and end fields accept HH:MM:SS.mmm, MM:SS.mmm or a plain number of seconds. The selected duration is calculated from the two values and updates while you drag; you can also type a duration to move the end time while keeping the start fixed. The end time must be greater than the start time, the start cannot be negative, and the end cannot pass the video's duration. If a value is invalid, the field is marked and the export button stays disabled until it is corrected.

The playhead and the timeline are two views of the same position. Pressing Set to playhead copies the current playback time into the start or end field. Dragging a handle moves the corresponding time field, and typing a time moves the handle. This keeps the visual selection and the numbers in sync.

Supported formats and browser limits

The tool reads the file with Mediabunny and decodes it with the browser's WebCodecs implementation. That means support depends on the browser, not on a fixed server-side list. In current Chrome and Edge, MP4 with H.264/AAC, WebM with VP8/VP9/Opus, and many MOV, M4V and MKV files work. Firefox and Safari expose different codec sets, so an option that appears in one browser may be missing in another.

Output is offered only when the browser can actually encode it. MP4 uses H.264 video and AAC audio. WebM uses VP9 video and Opus audio. If one format cannot be encoded at the video's resolution, it is hidden or disabled rather than offered as a broken option. If neither format can be encoded, the page explains that real trimming is unavailable for that file.

MOV and MKV inputs are decoded, but the download is MP4 or WebM. The page says so next to the format selector. Renaming a WebM file to .mp4 would be misleading, so the tool always uses the extension and MIME type that match the bytes it writes.

Quality, re-encoding and cut precision

A precise trim usually requires re-encoding. Video is stored in groups of frames that depend on each other, and a cut in the middle of a group cannot simply be copied out without decoding. This tool decodes the selected frames, re-encodes them, and muxes the result. It keeps the original display resolution and aspect ratio, and it keeps the audio track instead of silently dropping it.

Because the video is re-encoded, trimming is not lossless. The High, Balanced and Smaller file options change the encoder's quality target. High keeps more detail and produces a larger file; Smaller file compresses more aggressively. Re-encoding a very short clip can even produce a file that is larger than the source, especially if the source was already highly compressed.

The cut lands on the nearest decoded frame. At 30 fps that is about 33 milliseconds; at 60 fps it is about 17 milliseconds. The exact boundary can differ by up to one frame from the number you typed. Audio is trimmed at audio-sample boundaries, which are much finer. For a cut that must land on an exact frame number, use a frame-accurate editor; this tool is designed for fast, honest trimming in the browser.

Remove mode and joined output

Remove mode drops every decoded frame whose timestamp falls inside the selected range, trims audio samples that cross the boundaries, and shifts the remaining timestamps together. The result plays as one continuous file: the material before the selection is followed immediately by the material after it. Audio stays synchronized because the same time shift is applied to both tracks.

Removing a section from the very beginning or the very end is supported. Removing the entire video is not, because the output would contain no media; the export button explains that and stays disabled. As with keep mode, the join is frame-accurate to the nearest decoded frame rather than to an exact millisecond.

What this tool does not do

This is a trimmer, not a full editor. It does not add filters, transitions, subtitles, text, music or multiple clips. It does not crop or resize the frame, and it does not upload the video for AI processing. Those jobs belong in separate tools. Keeping the scope narrow makes the trim workflow faster and the result easier to predict.

Privacy and local processing

Your video is decoded, trimmed and encoded inside this browser tab. There is no upload, no account, no paid API and no server-side copy. Temporary object URLs are released when you remove the video, trim another file or leave the page. The export worker is closed when it is no longer needed, which releases its decoder and encoder resources.

Very large files are limited by device memory rather than by a server quota. The page accepts files up to 2 GB, but a long 4K video can exhaust memory on a phone. For the most reliable result, trim a shorter clip or close other memory-heavy tabs first. HDR and 10-bit sources may be converted to standard 8-bit video by the browser encoder; the tool does not claim to preserve a bit depth that the encoder cannot write.

Frequently asked questions

Does the video trimmer upload my video?

No. The video is decoded, trimmed and re-encoded in your browser. It is not sent to LetitEdit or to a third-party service, and no account is required.

Which video formats can I trim?

MP4, M4V, MOV, WebM and MKV are accepted when this browser can decode their video and audio codecs. Unsupported or corrupted files are rejected with a clear message.

Will the trimmed video keep the original resolution?

Yes. The tool keeps the original display width, height and aspect ratio. It does not resize, crop or letterbox the video.

Is trimming lossless or frame-accurate?

The video is re-encoded, so trimming is not a lossless stream copy. Cuts land on the nearest decoded frame, which is usually within one frame of the requested time. Audio is trimmed at audio-sample boundaries.

Can I remove a middle section and join the rest?

Yes. Remove mode drops the selected range and shifts the remaining video and audio together, producing a joined file. The cut points follow the same nearest-frame precision as keep mode.

Why does my MOV or MKV download as MP4?

The tool writes MP4 and WebM output. A MOV or MKV input can be decoded and trimmed, but the download uses MP4 or WebM so the file plays reliably in browsers and editors. The page states this before export.

Does the tool work on phones?

Yes, the layout is a single column on small screens with touch-friendly timeline handles and large time fields. Actual export speed depends on the phone's decoder and encoder support.

Is there a file size limit?

The tool accepts files up to 2 GB, but the practical limit is the memory available on your device. Very large or very long videos may be slow or may fail to load. Short clips are the most reliable.
