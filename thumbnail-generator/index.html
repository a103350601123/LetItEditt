How to use Thumbnail Generator

  1. Choose a verified preset such as YouTube 1280×720 or Instagram post 1080×1080, or enter a custom canvas size. On a phone, use the Preview and Controls switch to move between the large canvas and the editing panels.
  2. Upload up to eight photos; each stays a separate image layer. Drag a layer on the canvas to move it, pull a corner handle to resize, or use the rotate handle. The layer panel can reorder, hide, lock, duplicate and delete any element.
  3. Type in any language: direction is detected from the first strong character, with Auto, LTR and RTL controls. Font options change to stacks with known coverage for the dominant script. Add shapes, emoji stickers and original templates, then adjust colours, outlines and shadows.
  4. Use zoom, fit, fullscreen, safe-area and rule-of-thirds guides while designing; guides never appear in the export. Undo or redo, save or reopen a local project, then choose PNG, JPG or WebP and download at the exact canvas size.

In-depth guide

Why exact dimensions matter

A thumbnail is the first thing a viewer sees, and a canvas that is even slightly off the platform size can be cropped, padded or softened before it appears in a feed. This generator starts from the published pixel dimensions for each supported placement, so the file you download matches the frame you designed. YouTube thumbnails use 1280 by 720 pixels. Shorts, Reels, Stories and TikTok use a vertical 1080 by 1920 frame. A Facebook link post is 1200 by 630, a Facebook cover is 851 by 315, a LinkedIn banner is 1584 by 396, a standard X card is 1200 by 675 and a Pinterest Pin is 1000 by 1500. Each option shows its size in the selector before you choose it.

Working at the target size also avoids surprise interpolation. If you design at a smaller size and let a platform scale the result upward, text edges become blurred. Here the preview canvas is the export canvas, and PNG, JPG or WebP encoding preserves the exact pixel grid. You can still pick a custom size for a platform that changes its requirements later.

Layering without a heavyweight design app

The editor treats every headline, shape, sticker and uploaded photo as a separate layer. Add a layer from the current controls, give it a name, and it appears in the layer list where you can select, reorder, hide, lock, duplicate or delete it. Each layer keeps its own position, size, rotation, opacity, blend mode, font, colour, outline, shadow and alignment. Dragging on the preview moves the selected layer, corner handles resize it and a rotation handle turns it; pointer events make the same gestures work with a mouse, pen or finger. Nothing is flattened while you edit, so text stays text and photos stay independent images.

Alignment guides appear while dragging and the alignment buttons snap the active layer to the left, centre, right, top, middle or bottom of the canvas. Optional platform safe-area guides show the region likely to stay visible in a feed. This makes it straightforward to build a repeatable layout: place a photo, centre a headline, add a small badge in a corner, then duplicate the file and swap only the photograph for the next video. On a phone, the canvas takes priority and the controls sit behind a Preview/Controls switch, with zoom, fit and expand actions so small details remain inspectable without shrinking the output.

Original templates and safe artwork

Five starter templates are included: a bold headline, a quote card, a sale badge, a split-photo composition and a multilingual example. They are built from ordinary canvas rectangles, gradients, strokes and text, so nothing is licensed from a third party. Applying a template replaces the layer composition but keeps your uploaded photos, and every text layer remains editable. The sticker row uses standard Unicode emoji characters provided by your device font. You remain responsible for the images you upload, but the tool never supplies a copyrighted character, logo or trending graphic on your behalf.

Background choices include a two-colour gradient, a solid colour and transparency for layouts that will be composited later. A plain-background removal option is available for studio-style photos. It compares edge pixels with the corner colour and clears matching regions to transparent. Because it is deterministic and local rather than a neural network, flat backdrops work well and textured scenes may need a different source image.

Readable text at small sizes

Thumbnails are usually viewed at a fraction of their native size. A headline that looks elegant at full scale can disappear in a recommendation row. Use the font size, outline and shadow controls to preserve contrast, and keep important words away from the very edge of the frame because feeds may round or overlay the corners. The live preview uses the selected font stack, so what you see matches the downloaded file on the same device.

Text can be a single-line label or a multi-line caption with explicit line breaks. The editor detects the dominant script and offers font stacks with known coverage for Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Bengali, Gurmukhi, Tamil, Thai, Chinese, Japanese and Korean; an incompatible font is replaced with a suggested one and the status area says so. Direction follows Unicode first-strong rules, with an Auto, LTR or RTL override for Urdu, Arabic, Persian and mixed Latin script lines. Characters are never reversed by hand, and wrapping uses word and grapheme segmentation so CJK text and emoji sequences survive. Start, centre and end alignment apply within the layer box, while rotation lets you create angled badges. If a font is unavailable on another device, the browser falls back to the generic family named in the stack; exporting as PNG freezes the rendered pixels so the design remains stable when shared. Automated tests cover the major scripts, but no tool can guarantee identical rendering on every operating system.

Undo, history and careful export

Design work is iterative, so every structural action stores one snapshot. Adding, updating, duplicating, reordering or deleting a layer, changing the canvas size or background, applying a template or changing adjustments can all be stepped back with Undo and forward with Redo. You can also save the whole composition as a local JSON project and reopen it later; images are embedded as data URLs in that file, so keep it private if the artwork is confidential. Reset releases all temporary image URLs and restores the default canvas.

Before downloading, choose the format that fits the destination. PNG keeps transparency and sharp edges for text-heavy designs. JPG produces smaller files for photo-led thumbnails, and WebP offers a modern compromise where the browser supports it. The quality slider only affects lossy formats, and JPG flattens transparency onto white. Guides and selection handles live on a separate overlay, so the exported file contains only your artwork at the exact canvas dimensions. A downloaded thumbnail is a new file; your original photographs are never overwritten or sent anywhere.

Privacy and honest limitations

Everything runs in the browser. Uploaded images live only in memory and in short-lived object URLs that are revoked when you change or reset them. There is no account, no remote rendering service and no paid API. That design keeps private or unreleased artwork on your device, but it also means the editor cannot fetch stock photos, recognise faces or invent imagery for you.

This is a layout tool, not an AI image generator. It will not fabricate a scene from a text prompt, and no fake generation feature is presented. If you need illustrative artwork, create or license it yourself and bring it in as an image layer. What the generator does provide is precise sizing, movable layers, real adjustments and a predictable export so your own material looks its best.

Frequently asked questions

Are the social media dimensions accurate?

Yes. Each preset stores the documented pixel size for that placement, for example 1280×720 for a YouTube thumbnail, 1080×1920 for Reels and Stories, 1200×630 for a Facebook post and 1000×1500 for a Pinterest Pin. The canvas is never silently resized on export.

Can I use trending templates or characters?

No copyrighted artwork is bundled. The included templates are original arrangements of text, borders, gradients, simple shapes and standard system emoji. Use your own licensed images or artwork you have permission to reuse.

Can I use Urdu, Arabic, Chinese or other languages?

Yes. Text is stored as Unicode and rendered with script-aware font stacks and automatic direction from the first strong character. You can override direction with Auto, LTR or RTL for Urdu, Arabic, Persian, Hebrew and mixed-language lines. We verify the requested scripts in automated tests, but coverage also depends on the fonts installed on your device; a missing Noto font falls back to a system family. Characters are never manually reversed.

Does the downloaded file match the preview?

Yes. Export reuses the same painting routine at the native canvas size. Selection handles, alignment guides and safe-area guides are drawn on a separate overlay and are never encoded into the downloaded PNG, JPG or WebP. JPG flattens transparency onto white; PNG preserves it.

Can I move and resize text on a phone?

Yes. The mobile workspace gives the canvas the largest area and puts controls behind a Preview/Controls switch. Layer dragging, corner resizing and rotation use pointer events, which work with a finger or a mouse, and the canvas keeps its exact output dimensions even when the on-screen preview is scaled. Numeric sliders and arrow-key nudging give precise positioning.

Does background removal use AI?

No. The optional removal tool is a local colour-flood filter that clears plain backgrounds connected to the image edges. It works well on studio backdrops and flat colours, but detailed scenes need manual masking in a dedicated editor.

Is anything uploaded?

Nothing is uploaded. Image decoding, drawing and encoding all happen in your browser with temporary object URLs that are released when you reset or leave the page.
