      In-depth guide
      
What rule-based checking means

      
A rule-based checker does not understand your text the way a person does. Instead, it compares each word with a local word list and applies a small set of hand-written patterns for the mistakes that appear most often in English prose. When a pattern matches, the tool records the exact character range, the original text, a proposed replacement and a short explanation. You see those findings as highlights over the original text and as a list of suggestions underneath.

      
This design has a useful property: the result is predictable. The same input and the same style emphasis always produce the same suggestions, and every suggestion can be traced to a dictionary entry or a named rule. Nothing is generated, paraphrased or invented. The corrected copy is simply your own text with only the changes you chose to accept, applied from the end of the document backwards so earlier character offsets stay valid.

      
The categories it checks

      
Spelling covers a curated set of frequently confused and commonly misspelled English words, such as their versus there, its versus it is, affect versus effect, and words like accomodate or occured. These are high-value targets because they survive spell-check in a surprising number of drafts: every candidate is a real word, so a generic dictionary check would not flag it.

      
Grammar rules look for repeated words, missing spaces after punctuation, extra spaces before punctuation, a missing capital at the start of a sentence and the common contraction confusions involving apostrophes. Repeated-word detection catches accidental doubles like the the or to to, which are easy to miss when rereading your own writing. Spacing rules catch the small but distracting errors that appear when text is pasted between applications.

      
Clarity suggestions are deliberately conservative. They point at wordy phrases that have a shorter common equivalent and at obvious filler, but they never rewrite a sentence. Style emphasis changes which suggestions are shown: an academic emphasis keeps formal phrasing, while a simple-English emphasis favours shorter alternatives. Choosing a style never changes your text on its own; it only affects the suggestions offered for review.

      
Why the scope is English only

      
Spelling dictionaries, grammar patterns and style guidance are language specific. An English word list would flag perfectly correct words in French, Spanish or German, and English sentence rules do not apply to languages with different word order or no spaces between words. Rather than produce confidently wrong advice, this tool states that it checks English only. If you need another language, a dedicated checker for that language is the honest choice.

      
Even within English, local rules have clear limits. They cannot judge whether an argument is convincing, whether a tone suits its audience, or whether a passive construction was intentional. They do not understand context, irony or technical vocabulary, and they cannot detect a correctly spelled word used in the wrong sense. Treat every highlight as a question to answer, not an instruction to obey.

      
Privacy and how to use the results

      
All work happens on your device. The page loads no dictionary service, calls no translation endpoint and uses no paid API. Text you paste stays in the browser session and is never transmitted. That matters for drafts, student work, legal notes and anything else you would rather not upload to a third-party service.

      
The safest workflow is to check a finished draft, read each suggestion in context, accept only the ones you agree with, then proofread the corrected copy once more. Use the highlighted view to find a problem and the suggestion list to understand why it was flagged. Rejecting all suggestions restores the original text, and the corrected copy can be copied or downloaded as a plain text file.

      
What this tool is not

      
It is not a full natural-language-processing system, not an AI writing assistant and not a replacement for an editor. It does not analyse semantics, does not restructure paragraphs, does not generate alternatives beyond the fixed suggestions shown, and does not claim to improve your style in ways it cannot measure. Any page that promised those things from a small local rule set would be overstating what it can do.

      
The value here is a fast, private first pass that catches the mechanical errors people make most often. Used that way, it saves time and leaves the decisions in your hands.

  





  

    

      
Frequently asked questions

      

  Is this an AI writing tool?
  
No. It is an explicitly rule-based, English-only checker. It uses local dictionaries and grammar rules, not a language model, and it does not rewrite your prose or invent new sentences.



  Does it send my text anywhere?
  
No. Every check runs in your browser. There is no external, paid or third-party API, and no text is uploaded to a server.



  Will it catch every mistake?
  
No. It covers common spelling errors, frequent grammar patterns, repeated words, spacing and a few clarity rules. It is an aid to proofreading, not a replacement for a human reader or a specialist editor.



  Does it work in languages other than English?
  
No. The dictionaries and rules are English only. Other languages will produce unreliable suggestions, so the tool states that scope plainly.
