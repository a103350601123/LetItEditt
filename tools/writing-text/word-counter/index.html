  1. Type or paste text, or load a plain-text or Markdown file.

In-depth guide

What the numbers mean

Words are tokens containing at least one Unicode letter or number. Characters are grapheme clusters rather than raw code units, which is why an accented letter or a flag emoji counts as a single character. Letters count Unicode letter code points, and unique words are case-insensitive.

Reading time assumes 238 words per minute and speaking time assumes 150 words per minute. Both show at least one second when any word is present.

Keyword density rules

Density is calculated for one, two or three word phrases inside sentence-like segments. Common words can be excluded, numeric-only single words are skipped, and phrases must appear at least twice. Results are sorted by frequency and then alphabetically, with the top ten shown.

Frequently asked questions

How do you count words?

Text is split into lines and tokens; punctuation at token edges is removed and tokens containing at least one letter or number count as words. Languages that do not use spaces are handled with browser word segmentation where available.

Are emoji counted as one character?

Yes. Character counts use grapheme clusters via Intl.Segmenter when available, so a joined emoji sequence is counted as one visible character.

Does it save my text?

No. This build keeps text in memory for the current page only. Closing or refreshing the page clears it.

How accurate is sentence counting?

It is a heuristic. Common abbreviations are handled, but unusual punctuation can still be counted differently from a human reader.
