In-depth guide

Replacing a background without a green screen

A talking-head video often needs a tidier setting than the room it was recorded in. Traditionally that means a green screen, careful lighting and a separate keying step. This tool takes a different route: it uses MediaPipe person segmentation to estimate which pixels belong to the person, then fills the rest with a colour or an image you provide. The work happens locally in your browser, and your video is never uploaded. The model is not loaded automatically. You read a short notice and opt in, at which point the model and its WebAssembly support files are downloaded to your device. That disclosure matters: the first run needs those files and takes longer, while later runs may reuse the browser cache. If you do not want the download, you can simply not enable the feature.

What person segmentation can and cannot do

Person segmentation is a specific task. The model looks at each frame and produces an estimate of the person-shaped region in the foreground. It is not a general background eraser and it does not understand every object in a scene. A pet beside the speaker, a handheld product or a chair will normally be treated as background and replaced along with the room. There is no brush here for teaching it about an arbitrary object. Results also depend on the footage: an evenly lit person against a contrasting background gives the cleanest boundary, while motion blur, similar colours in front and behind, and crowded frames make the estimate harder. Check several frames across the clip rather than judging the preview from one lucky still, because a mask can hold steady for a second and then flicker during movement.

Choosing a replacement background

A solid colour is the simplest option and often the most useful. It produces a clean, predictable frame that works well for explainer videos or profile clips, and it makes mistakes around the edges easier to see before you export. An uploaded image adds context: a blurred office, a plain wall texture or a branded backdrop. Choose something without a busy pattern directly behind the person's head, because the edge of the mask will be most noticeable there. Match the lighting direction of the original recording where you can; a person lit from the left pasted onto a background lit from the right looks pasted on even when the mask is accurate. Preview the beginning, middle and end of the clip after changing the background. Anything that looks wrong in the preview will look wrong in the download, so adjust before exporting rather than hoping the final render will improve it.

Transparent WebM and browser support

Some workflows need the person with no replacement at all, so the subject can be composited over another video later. That requires a video format with an alpha channel, and browser support for encoding alpha is uneven. Where it is supported, the tool can offer a transparent WebM download. Where it is not, the option is absent or the export is unavailable; that is a real capability limit, not a setting you missed. A solid colour or image background is the reliable alternative and can be swapped later in a full editor if you have the right source files. Even with alpha support, expect the same edge limitations discussed earlier: segmentation is an estimate, and fine hair or fast motion can produce fringing. Check the transparent export against a checkerboard or contrasting colour before you build a project around it.

Performance, privacy and cleanup

Segmentation is more demanding than a simple filter because every frame has to be analysed. Short clips work best. A long or high-resolution video can take noticeable time, so keep the tab active and avoid running several heavy tools at once. The model download is cached by your browser, which makes subsequent runs faster, and it can be cleared through your normal site data settings if you want to reclaim space. Your video itself is held in memory while the page is open; closing or reloading discards it, and nothing is uploaded at any stage. Download the finished file before you leave. Keep the original recording as your master copy, and treat the result as a useful local composite rather than a perfect studio cut. Clear lighting, a steady subject and a modest clip length will do more for quality than any setting.

Frequently asked questions

Does this upload my video?

No. Segmentation and rendering run in your browser. Your video is not sent to LetitEdit or to a vision service. However, the MediaPipe model and WebAssembly support files must be downloaded to your device when you first enable the feature; that is a download to you, not an upload of your media.

Why do I have to opt in?

The model is an extra component with its own download size and processing cost. Opting in makes that clear before anything is fetched and lets you use the rest of the page without downloading files you do not need.

Does it remove objects as well as people?

No. The model is trained for person segmentation. It identifies a person in the foreground and leaves everything else as background. It will not reliably isolate a car, a pet or a product, and it cannot be aimed at an arbitrary object by painting a selection.

Can I get a transparent video?

Sometimes. A transparent WebM needs browser support for encoding an alpha channel, and not all browsers offer it. If the option is unavailable or the result looks wrong, use a solid colour or image background instead. The page does not pretend alpha support exists everywhere.

Why are the edges around hair imperfect?

Segmentation estimates which pixels belong to a person, and fine detail such as loose hair, motion blur or semi-transparent fabric is difficult. Expect some fringing or slight jitter, particularly in fast movement. Clear lighting and a strong contrast between person and background usually give a cleaner mask.

What happens to the model and my working files?

Downloaded model files are cached by your browser for later use and can be removed through normal site data controls. The video itself is held only in memory while the page is open; reloading or closing discards it. Download your result before leaving, and keep the original file as your master.
