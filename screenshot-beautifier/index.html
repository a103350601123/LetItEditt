In-depth guide

Why raw screenshots need a little work

A screenshot dropped straight into a document often looks accidental. Its edges butt against the page, the surrounding colours clash, and there is nothing to tell the eye where the capture begins. A beautifier addresses those presentation problems without asking you to open a design application. You load the image, choose a background for the space around it, add padding so it does not crowd the edges, and optionally soften the corners and add a drop shadow. Text and filters are available for emphasis, and a decorative title bar can make the capture look like a window. The result is a clean PNG that reads as deliberate rather than pasted in a hurry. The controls are live, so you can compare a few styles before committing to a download.

Starting with the background

The background is the area behind and around your screenshot, and it sets the tone for everything else. A gradient gives a modern look and works well in presentations and social posts. A solid colour is predictable and easy to match to a brand palette or document theme. Transparent leaves the surrounding area see-through, which is useful when the result will be placed on a page that already has its own colour or texture. A pattern adds visual interest but can distract from dense text, so use it sparingly. Choose the background before fine-tuning padding and shadow, because those settings interact with what sits behind the image. A dark background, for example, makes a subtle shadow harder to see. If in doubt, start with a solid colour and switch to gradient or pattern once the framing is settled.

Padding, corners and shadow

Padding is the space between the screenshot and the edge of the finished canvas. Even a modest amount gives the image breathing room and prevents it from looking cramped. Rounded corners soften the hard rectangle of a raw capture; a small radius reads as polished, while a large one can start to crop visual information at the corners. Shadow adds depth by separating the screenshot from the background, but it needs room to render — if the padding is too tight, the shadow gets cut off and looks like a hard edge. Tune these three controls together in the preview. Increase padding when the shadow disappears, reduce the radius if corners look clipped, and keep the overall effect subtle when the screenshot contains small text. Small adjustments are easier to judge than dramatic ones, especially when the capture includes fine lettering.

Filters, text and title bars

Filters can shift the mood of a capture. A slight desaturation reduces visual noise, while a warmer or cooler tint can help the screenshot sit alongside other images. They are enhancements, not repairs: no filter recovers text that was captured at too low a resolution. A text overlay lets you label the image or point out a detail, but keep it short and place it where it will not cover important content. The Mac and Windows-style title bars are decorative frames with familiar dots or buttons above the capture. They make a screenshot look like a tidy window and are useful in documentation. They do not alter the application shown underneath, so do not treat them as evidence of a particular operating system. Place text in the padding area rather than over the interface whenever possible.

Export, privacy and troubleshooting

The finished composition downloads as a PNG, which keeps edges, text and shadows crisp. Editing happens in your browser: the screenshot is not uploaded to LetitEdit or to any external service. If the exported image looks cropped, check the padding first, because a shadow needs space to fade out. If the background seems to vanish, you may have selected transparent and are viewing the PNG against a similarly coloured page. A title bar that looks oversized on a small capture can be left off without affecting the rest of the layout. Very large screenshots may take a moment to render; wait for the preview to settle before exporting. Reloading the page clears the current composition so you can start again. Keeping a copy of the original screenshot also lets you try a completely different style later.

Frequently asked questions

Is my screenshot uploaded?

No. The composition is assembled in your browser, and the screenshot is never sent to LetitEdit or a third-party service.

Can I make the background transparent?

Yes. Choose the transparent background option when you want the padding around the screenshot to stay see-through in the exported PNG.

What do the Mac and Windows title bars do?

They add a decorative window bar above the capture with the familiar dot or button styling. They are visual framing only and do not change the screenshot content beneath.

Will this fix a blurry or low-resolution screenshot?

No. Filters and framing can make a capture look tidier, but they cannot add detail that was not captured. Start with the sharpest original you have.

My export has a hard edge or the shadow is cut off. Why?

Increase the padding so there is enough room around the screenshot for the shadow to render. Also check that a transparent background is not being viewed against a similarly coloured page.
