How to use AI Video-to-Shorts

  1. Load a video your browser can decode and keep the tab active.
  2. Let the tool measure loudness and motion to suggest candidate highlight windows.
  3. Optionally opt in to local Whisper to rank candidates by transcript density.
  4. Review each suggested window and adjust its start or end if needed.
  5. Choose the vertical framing and export a 9:16 clip for each selection.
  6. Download the SRT file for a window when transcript ranking is enabled.

In-depth guide

Finding candidate moments from measurable signals

Turning a long recording into short vertical clips starts with a simple question: which parts are worth a second look? This tool answers with signals it can actually measure in your browser. Audio loudness rises during animated conversation, applause or a sudden reaction. Picture motion changes when someone stands up, demonstrates something or cuts to a new shot. Analysing those patterns produces candidate highlight windows, each of which you can preview and adjust before export. Nothing is uploaded. The word AI is used carefully here: the default analysis is signal processing, not a system that understands your topic or predicts an audience response. The candidates are starting points for your own judgement, and the page says so rather than implying that a number can replace watching the clip.

The optional transcript and its fallback

You can extend the signal-based analysis with a locally running Whisper tiny model loaded through the browser. When you opt in, the model file is downloaded to your device, roughly 75 to 80 MB on first use, and speech recognition produces a transcript with timing information. Windows with denser speech can then rank higher among the candidates, and an exported clip can be accompanied by an SRT subtitle file. This is genuinely useful for interviews, talks and podcasts where the best short moments are packed with speech. It is not required. If you skip the model, the loudness and motion suggestions still work and you can still export vertical clips; you simply do not get transcript-based ranking or subtitles. The fallback is explicit so you know exactly which features are active and which download you have avoided.

Reviewing and adjusting each window

Automatic suggestions are useful precisely because they are fast, but they can be wrong in interesting ways. A loud segment might be a door slamming rather than a punchline. Fast motion might come from someone adjusting a camera. A transcript may rank a dense passage that makes no sense without the preceding sentence. For that reason, every candidate should be watched before export. Play from a little before the suggested start so you can hear the setup, and let the clip run past the end to see whether the reaction completes. Adjust the boundaries where the sentence or action naturally finishes. Short clips live or die by their opening and ending: a half-second of dead air at the front or a cut in the middle of a word is much more noticeable in a vertical format than in a long timeline. Keep the candidates that stand on their own and discard the rest.

Cropping to a vertical frame

A 9:16 export is shaped for a phone held upright, which is a very different frame from most source footage. The tool crops the picture to that ratio, and what you lose matters. A centred speaker with a plain background usually survives the crop well. A wide scene with important detail at both edges will not, and you may decide that the moment is unsuitable rather than accept a broken composition. Preview the vertical result rather than trusting the landscape view. Text and overlays placed near the edges of the original may disappear completely. If a clip depends on a graph, a second person or a caption at the side, consider a different moment or prepare a purpose-built version in the full editor. Cropping is a geometric operation; the tool cannot invent picture content outside the original frame.

Privacy, performance and honest expectations

All analysis and encoding happen locally. Your video is not sent to LetitEdit, and enabling Whisper downloads a model to your device instead of uploading your media. That model is cached by the browser and can be removed through site data controls; the video itself stays in memory until you close or reload the page. Download every clip and subtitle file you want to keep before leaving. Short, clear sources work best. A long video takes time to analyse, and several vertical exports mean several encoding passes, so keep the tab active and close unrelated heavy tabs. Export options depend on the encoders in your browser, and unavailable formats are reported honestly. Finally, treat this as an assistant for finding possibilities. It can save you scrubbing through an hour of footage, but only you can judge whether a moment is interesting, coherent and appropriate to share.

Frequently asked questions

What does the AI actually do?

By default it analyses measurable signals: audio loudness and changes in the picture. Peaks in those signals suggest moments worth reviewing. If you opt in, a locally running Whisper model also provides a transcript, and windows with denser speech can rank higher. It is a suggestion system, not an understanding of what makes a clip go viral.

What happens if I do not enable the model?

The signal-based suggestions still work. You get candidate windows from loudness and motion, and you can export vertical clips from them. There is no transcript and no SRT file in that mode. The fallback is a real feature, not a disabled tool.

Is anything uploaded?

No. Analysis, cropping and encoding run in your browser. If you enable Whisper, its model files are downloaded to your device; your video is not sent anywhere. Model and media handling are disclosed before you opt in.

How large is the model download?

The Whisper tiny model used for captions is roughly 75 to 80 MB with its support files, fetched on first use and usually cached afterwards. You can skip it entirely and still use the loudness and motion workflow.

Will the suggestions always pick the best moments?

No. Loud moments can be arguments or noise, and motion can come from a camera shake. Transcript density favours busy speech, which may not be the most interesting part. Treat every window as a candidate and watch it before exporting. Your judgement is the final filter.

How is vertical framing handled?

The tool crops to a 9:16 frame. Composition is a trade-off: a wide shot may lose its edges, while a centred speaker usually crops well. Check each preview before export. Processing depends on browser encoders, so an unavailable format is reported rather than replaced with a fake download.
